When planning a private transport itinerary across Rajasthan, many travellers make the mistake of choosing a vehicle based solely on passenger seat counts while completely forgetting about their luggage. 

A standard Sedan might comfortably seat three adults for a 30-minute city drop, but packing three adults alongside four large check-in suitcases for a 5-hour highway journey to Jodhpur creates a highly cramped, uncomfortable ride.

### 1. The "Suitcase Rule" for Sedans and SUVs
In Indian transport fleets, vehicle boot spaces vary significantly between models:

* **Sedans (Swift Dzire / Toyota Etios):** The dedicated rear boot is built to hold **2 standard medium hard-shell suitcases** plus 1 or 2 soft backpacks. If you have a third passenger with a third large hard-shell suitcase, that bag will have to sit inside the cabin between passengers.
* **SUVs (Maruti Ertiga / Toyota Rumion):** When all 3 rows of seats are upright to accommodate 5 or 6 passengers, the remaining rear boot space drops drastically. If you are travelling with 5 adults and heavy luggage, ask for a roof carrier or upgrade to a Prime SUV (Innova Crysta) where legroom and boot dimensions are significantly wider.

### 2. Why Groups Should Never Split Into Multiple Cars
If you are travelling with an extended family of 8 to 12 people, booking three separate taxis often seems like an easy option at first. However, on long outstation routes across Rajasthan, splitting up creates unnecessary complications:
* Vehicles can get separated in city traffic or toll plazas.
* Coordination for meal and restroom stops becomes difficult.
* The combined cost of three separate cars and driver allowances is often higher than hiring a single **12-Seater Luxury Tempo Traveller**.

### 3. Sharing Accurate Information for an Quick Quote
To ensure your transport provider allocates the correct vehicle category from day one, always share the exact number of **large hard-shell bags** you will be carrying when submitting your initial travel request form.
